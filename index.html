      资Investing

      Founder integrity comes first

      Life is relationships, and backing a company is a long one. The first thing I evaluate is founder integrity: whether a founder tells the truth when the numbers are bad and keeps commitments to patients, partners, and co-founders. Then I look for a buyer and a payment path.

      I build a thesis through primary conversations with clinicians, operators, payers, and buyers, then map each concept to who pays and why. I test founder and market fit, business model, and market size, and I work the unit economics in the forms healthcare uses: shared savings, PMPM, and value-based contracts.

      At Fusion Fund, a $120M seed and Series A fund, I screened 30 founders in 8 weeks while a full-time Stanford GSB student, and sourced, diligenced, and championed the fund's largest proposed healthcare investment, an AI-enabled rapid diagnostics company. Today I advise startups at the Stanford Byers Center for Biodesign.

      • Care delivery that is AI-native from the first workflow.
      • Revenue cycle and coding automation inside the tools clinicians already use.
      • Employer and payer products that tie every intervention to outcomes and cost.
      • Diagnostics that put AI in a clinical decision with a human reviewing it.

      Two feet steeped in East & West, one heart examining the cosmos.

      My mother's homeland is China and my father's is America. I aim to be a bridge between the two, and healthcare is where I think the bridge matters most. I have worked in a Chinese hospital ward, in a Brooklyn public hospital, and inside one of the largest U.S. insurers. Each showed me a different answer to the same question: who gets care, and who pays.

      1. Keep a human in the loop on clinical decisions.The agent never diagnoses or ranks providers; every message is screened for red flags, and clinical questions go to a nurse.
      2. Prototype in days before committing to the build.The first working agent took ten days and decided whether the full build was worth it.
      3. Tie every intervention to an outcome.Shared-savings reporting rests on linking each intervention to clinical outcomes and cost.
      4. Time the real workflow before setting a target.Five timed patient journeys set the targets: same-day consent, nurse onboarding under 15 minutes.
